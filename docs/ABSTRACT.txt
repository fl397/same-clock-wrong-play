Same Clock, Wrong Play: Auditing NBA Tracking–Play-by-Play Alignment

Introduction
Join the public 2015-16 SportVU corpus to play-by-play at equal game-clock values and spacing appears to be a shooting-efficiency lever: effective field-goal percentage climbs from 0.478 in the tightest spacing quartile to 0.572 in the widest (27,563 shots). Align the clocks and the gradient vanishes (0.524, 0.498, 0.491, 0.505; 64,764 shots). As ismayc/tracking-study first showed on 10 games, play-by-play timestamps lag the tracking clock, so an equal-clock join lands on the wrong play: seconds that usually belong to the following possession, often to the other team. That work introduced shot-based offset calibration with turnovers held out and demonstrated the spacing/eFG artifact; we apply and extend its strategy to the full corpus with season-scale validation, a downstream-consequence audit, and a reproducible correction resource.

Methods
All 632 games of the public corpus were joined to stats.nba.com PlayByPlayV3; 631 were calibratable. Following ismayc/tracking-study, a nearest-player heuristic labels possession per frame, the lead maximizing shot-team agreement is estimated per game, and 16,943 turnovers are held out. We compared a fixed +4 s shift with game-specific calibration, classified each game's agreement curve (sharp, plateau, multimodal, drifting), audited downstream state assignment, and separated clock misalignment from a second failure mode, stopped-clock frames inside possessions, with a frozen live-play classifier. Rules were committed before results; a fresh clone reproduced 25 games identically.

Results
An equal-clock join does not usually find the play it names: the two-second pre-shot window belongs to the other team 69.0% of the time, and 23.0% of 7,625 detected screens sit in a different possession. Aggregate mean speed fails to reveal the problem: on naive, unfiltered possessions it lands within 0.03 SD of the corrected, live-only value. Two opposite errors cancel: clock misalignment inflates speed by 0.22 SD while non-live frames (14.7% of frames) deflate it by 0.20 SD. Aggregate plausibility does not establish event-level validity. A fixed +4 s shift raises held-out turnover-team agreement from 0.393 to 0.942, at least as well as game-specific calibration (0.932) in every curve class, cuts wrong-team pre-shot windows to 3.5%, and lowers apparent screen onsets within 0.5 s of possession start from 27.7% to 4.7%. In a transition-offense analysis, naive timing preserved the broad association but corrupted the monotone numerical-recovery structure that corrected timing restores.

Conclusion
For this public 2015-16 SportVU/PBP corpus: add +4.0 s to the play-by-play clock before selecting tracking frames, carry the lower-confidence labels for multimodal (19.0%) and drifting (10.6%) games, filter non-live frames before computing movement metrics, and validate any join on held-out events. We release the correction rule, per-game quality labels, live-play filters, validation code and a fresh-clone record; the corrected corpus is withheld pending data rights. The +4 s constant is claimed for this corpus only, not for other seasons, providers, leagues or sports; what travels is the principle behind held-out correspondence checks whenever sports-data streams are joined: aggregate plausibility does not establish event-level validity.
